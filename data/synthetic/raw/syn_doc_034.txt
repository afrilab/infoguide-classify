Access Control Review and Coordination Update

Date: Early May
From: JPMorgan Chase & Co.
To: All System Administrators
Location: Central Office, Texas, USA

This memo provides an update on the ongoing review of access control practices across all systems within JPMorgan Chase & Co. The objective of this review is to ensure that access permissions are managed consistently and that all teams adhere to current identity and access management policies.

The review was initiated following observations made during recent system checks. It was identified that access rights in several environments may not fully reflect current role assignments. In particular, some accounts appear to retain permissions that are no longer required based on updated responsibilities.

During internal discussions, carter, m, who is responsible for identity management oversight, highlighted that certain entries in the system logs resemble structured identifiers but do not correspond to actual user records. These entries, often formatted in patterns such as "ID-45872" or "Usern Temp", may create confusion during manual audits.

The group also noted that communication between teams frequently relies on role-based references rather than explicit identification. Expressions such as "the admin", "the reviewer", or "the coordinator" are commonly used, which can make it difficult to trace accountability when reviewing access changes.

Key points addressed include:

- Identification of outdated or unnecessary access permissions
- Clarification of naming conventions used in system records
- Risks associated with ambiguous role-based references
- Need for improved traceability in access management processes

It was emphasized that all teams should verify current access rights and ensure that permissions align with actual responsibilities. Inconsistent application of access policies may increase the risk of unauthorized actions or audit findings.

JPMorgan Chase & Co. will provide updated guidelines outlining how permissions should be assigned and reviewed. These guidelines will include clearer definitions of roles and responsibilities, as well as recommendations for documenting access changes.

Action Items:

- carter, m to coordinate publication of updated access control guidelines
- System administrators to review existing permissions and remove unnecessary access
- Teams to document all access changes in accordance with updated standards

Additional Notes:

- A follow-up review will be conducted during the next audit phase
- Teams are expected to complete initial checks before that period

It was also discussed that some references used in internal communication may appear to indicate specific entities but are in fact generic labels. This distinction is important for ensuring that audit processes remain accurate and reliable.

For further clarification, system administrators are advised to consult internal documentation or contact the relevant unit responsible for access management.

The memo concludes with a reminder that maintaining clear and consistent access control practices is essential for both operational security and regulatory compliance.
