Mid-Year Financial Activity and Exposure Review

This report provides an analytical overview of financial activity and exposure trends observed within Helion Banking Corporation during the first half of 2025. The review focuses on transaction behavior, regional distribution of activity, and emerging patterns identified through internal monitoring processes.

The reporting period extends, with comparative observations drawn from activity recorded in the final quarter of 2024. Data used in this analysis was extracted from internal reporting systems and verified through standard reconciliation procedures prior to consolidation.

Helion Banking Corporation operates across several major financial centers, with transaction activity concentrated in İzmir, Kartal, and Maltepe. During the reporting period, İzmir recorded the highest overall transaction volume, followed by Kartal and Maltepe. These distributions were broadly consistent with prior reporting cycles.

A temporary increase in transaction frequency was observed during late March, with a peak identified. Initial analysis suggested that the increase was associated with elevated corporate payment activity. However, further review indicated that a portion of this activity involved repeated cross-border transfers executed within short time intervals.

Additional review conducted confirmed that similar transaction clusters had occurred intermittently since February 2025. Although individual transactions remained within approved thresholds, the recurrence of these patterns suggested a gradual shift in transaction behavior.

The analysis also considered exposure to cross-border transaction risk. While overall exposure levels remained within acceptable limits, data from April and May 2025 indicated a higher concentration of activity involving multiple counterparties across different jurisdictions. This trend was particularly visible in transactions processed through the Kartal unit.

During the validation process, the financial reporting team identified minor discrepancies in data submitted by the Maltepe office. These discrepancies were resolved prior to final reporting and did not materially affect the conclusions.

From a control perspective, no significant failures were identified. However, the persistence of clustered transaction patterns suggests that existing monitoring thresholds may require adjustment in future reporting cycles.
